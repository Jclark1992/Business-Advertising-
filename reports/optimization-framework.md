# Optimization Decision Framework

This is the rulebook the weekly reports and any ad-hoc analysis follow. It
exists so recommendations are consistent and defensible, not vibes-based.

## Objective

Generate more qualified laser hair removal bookings at the lowest
sustainable acquisition cost — not the most clicks. Particular emphasis on
women searching for Brazilian, underarm, and other laser hair removal
services in West Edmonton. Real conversion data overrides the initial
keyword/positioning assumptions in this repo once it's statistically
meaningful.

## What gets reviewed every cycle

Search terms, keywords, CTR, CPC, conversions, conversion rate, cost per
conversion/lead, geographic performance, device performance, ad performance,
landing-page relevance, search intent, and wasted spend.

## Core rules

1. **Sample size before action.** Never negative, pause, or reallocate
   budget based on a single day or a handful of clicks. A term with 3
   clicks and 0 conversions is not evidence of anything.
2. **Protect what's working.** Keywords/ad groups/campaigns demonstrably
   producing profitable conversions don't get touched just to "optimize"
   further — changes need a real reason.
3. **Intent over clicks.** A keyword getting clicks isn't automatically
   valuable. Weight commercial-intent terms ("laser hair removal edmonton,"
   "laser hair removal near me," "brazilian laser hair removal edmonton")
   above informational ones ("how does laser hair removal work," "does
   laser hurt," "how to prevent razor burn") — the latter are researchers,
   not bookers.
4. **Promote and prune search terms deliberately.** Strong, relevant,
   high-intent search terms get promoted to exact-match keywords. Irrelevant
   or wasteful ones become negative-keyword candidates — but always with
   the evidence (clicks, cost, conversions, timeframe) shown, not just a
   gut call.
5. **Reduce waste before increasing budget.** Don't chase traffic volume at
   the expense of lead quality.
6. **Compare across timeframes.** Where enough data exists, compare 7/14/30
   day windows to separate a real trend from a temporary blip before
   recommending a change.

## Format for any significant recommendation

Every budget, bidding, targeting, keyword, campaign, or conversion-tracking
change gets written up as:

1. **What was found** (with the actual numbers/timeframe)
2. **What's recommended**
3. **Why**
4. **Expected benefit**
5. **Potential risk**

Small, obvious housekeeping (e.g., adding a clearly irrelevant search term
as a negative) can be a plain checkbox without the full write-up — reserve
the 5-part format for anything that meaningfully shifts spend, targeting,
or structure.

## Reporting cadence

Weekly review is the default cadence (see root `README.md` and
`reports/README.md`). If there's nothing new to review — no new export, no
new data — the check-in says so briefly rather than manufacturing a report.
